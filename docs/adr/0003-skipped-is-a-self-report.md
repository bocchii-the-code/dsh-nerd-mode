# A skipped concept is a self-report, not evidence

At graph approval the learner may drop concepts they already know. The row is written as `skipped`, with no date and no passes.

The evidence rule forbids a self-report from changing state, but a strict reading leaves every topic starting at concept 1. A reader who already knows the basics then sits through material they do not need, and every later concept stays blocked until the tutor has taught it all. ChatGPT Study Mode answers this with a calibration question. Its weak point is that the answer becomes the level, even when the learner is wrong about what they know.

`skipped` takes the middle: it removes a concept from the queue without claiming knowledge. It satisfies prerequisites for steering, and any lesson that relies on it opens with one line of recap.

The cost is visible. A failed concept with a `skipped` prerequisite was built on an untested guess, so the prerequisite becomes `shaky`. `skipped` never becomes `fluent`, never carries a date, and never counts as a pass.

## Amendment, 2026-10-25

The learner may drop a concept at any time, not only at graph approval. The action is the same and so is the row: `skipped`, no date, no passes, and a note that records the reason as `self-report, not needed` or `self-report, done`.

The widening costs something. A learner who is tired can empty the queue without producing anything, and the only check left is a later failure on a dependent concept. That is accepted, because the alternative is a learner held inside a lecture they asked to leave.
